The Importance of Wetlands

Wetlands are areas where water covers the soil or remains near the surface for much of the year. Although they were once dismissed as useless or unhealthy places, scientists now recognize wetlands as some of the most productive ecosystems on Earth. They filter water by trapping pollutants and excess nutrients, and they provide habitat for birds, fish, insects, and amphibians.

Wetlands also help protect communities from floods. During heavy rain, they absorb and store large amounts of water, releasing it slowly over time. This reduces the force of floodwaters and helps prevent damage to nearby towns. When wetlands are drained or filled for construction, that natural protection disappears, and flooding can become more severe.

Despite their benefits, wetlands continue to be threatened by agriculture, housing, and industrial development. Conservationists argue that protecting wetlands is more cost-effective than repairing flood damage or building artificial water treatment systems. In many cities, planners are now restoring wetlands as part of parks and green infrastructure projects.
